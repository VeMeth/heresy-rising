# Astropath

> *The warp remembers. It leaves a stain on every soul who passes through another's sight.*

You are the veiled Astropath — a psyker whose gift has been conditioned by Inquisitorial practice to read the residue of the warp itself. Each night, you pick one operative and one tier of reading, paying self-drift, to learn who has touched that operative with a night action. You never learn *what* action they took, never their faction. Only names. Only visitors.

**Night action — Visitor History.** One target. One tier (T1, T2, or T3). The system reveals visitor names across the window and attribution detail your tier grants — see the table below.

| Action | Effect | Cost (you) | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visitor History — T1** | **One** name from tonight's visitors to your chosen operative. The Warp picks the name at random — you don't get to choose which visitor you see. No per-night attribution. | +3 drift | Nightly, when not crippled |
| **Visitor History — T2** | **All** names from visitors to your chosen operative across tonight and the night before, pooled (no per-night attribution). | +6 drift | Nightly, when not crippled |
| **Visitor History — T3** | **All** names from visitors to your chosen operative across tonight and the night before, WITH per-night attribution (who came which specific night). Flat +12 drift floor — never scales down at larger tables. | +12 drift | Nightly, when not crippled |

**Tier costs (Warp invoice):** +3 (T1), +6 (T2), or +12 (T3) per night you read. The T3 cost is a mandatory flat floor — it does not shrink on bigger rosters, unlike most tiered costs in this game.

**How the Warp picks at T1.** When you read at T1, the Warp surfaces one name from the list of players whose night actions targeted your operative tonight. You don't pick which one — the Warp does, at random. If five different visitors touched your operative tonight, you hear one of them. If nobody visited them, you hear "no one crossed their path tonight" and you still pay the +3 drift. **T1 is a cheap peek, not a reliable read.** It is the Astropath's daily oracle: you can run it every night without burning drift, but don't expect every night to give you the name you wanted.

**What the warp shows you:** the *names* of night actors who targeted your chosen operative. That is all.

- At **T1**, you hear one name from tonight's visitors, no attribution.
- At **T2**, you hear every name that touched the operative across tonight and the night before — but you can't tell which night each arrived.
- At **T3**, you hear every name, with the night attached. Two consecutive T3 reads on the same operative let you cross-reference and identify the same visitor across three nights.

**What you do *not* learn:** the target's role, faction, exact drift, *what action* each visitor took, or which faction they belong to. Pure names — nothing more.

**Cannot target self.** Self-read is not allowed. Reading your own warp-stain does not help the cell.

**When you are crippled** (cripple tier > 0), the engine silently rejects your action — no names, no drift cost, no error shown. This is the same silent-rejection convention as Chirurgeon, Arbitrator, and Novice-Psychic.

**Repeat targets allowed:** you can read the same operative on consecutive nights without penalty.

**Strategic note — the T1/T2 trade-off.** T2 nightly costs twice the drift of T1 nightly (+6 vs +3) and gives you a *full* visitor list instead of one random pick. The cell should escalate to T2 only when one random name isn't enough — when you need the whole picture. T1 is the daily bread; T2 is the escalation; T3 is the all-in climax.

**Play this role if:** you want precise visitor history at a high self-cost, you're comfortable never knowing *what* actions your targets took, and you accept that T1 is a dice roll — one random name, not a list.

**Loyalists should look for:** T2 and T3 are where the gold is. If you read at T2 and a Heretic appears on the list, you know they touched your operative *somewhere* in the last two nights — pin them with a T3 the next night to lock the exact night. T1 is for daily pattern-spotting: keep reading your most-watched operative nightly and the Warp will hand you one name per night. The same Heretic keeps appearing on your random picks? Strong signal.

**Heretics should look for:** the Astropath is expensive at T3 (+12 flat floor). Spam T3 and you'll be Orange by Round 4–5, then dead on a single T2+ Interrogator scan. Kill the Astropath early, or force them to drop to cheaper tiers. At T1 specifically, you can *defeat the read by varying your targets* — if you spread your visits across many different nights, the random T1 pick may never surface you.
